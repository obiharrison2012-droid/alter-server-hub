The Liturgical Year: seasons and meaning

The Roman Catholic liturgical year begins with the First Sunday of Advent. Dates vary from year to year. The calendar below gives the usual order and purpose of the seasons.

1. Advent

Usual colour: Violet or purple; rose on the Third Sunday (Gaudete Sunday) where used.

A season of hope, prayer and preparation for Christ’s coming, both at Christmas and at the end of time. The Advent wreath is a common devotion.

2. Christmas Time

Usual colour: White or gold.

Celebrates the birth of Jesus and the mystery of God becoming human. It includes Christmas, the Holy Family, Mary Mother of God, Epiphany and the Baptism of the Lord, according to the calendar.

3. Ordinary Time (first part)

Usual colour: Green.

Focuses on Jesus’ public ministry and growth in Christian discipleship. It runs after the Baptism of the Lord until before Ash Wednesday.

4. Lent

Usual colour: Violet or purple; rose may be used on the Fourth Sunday (Laetare Sunday).

A 40-day season of prayer, fasting and almsgiving preparing for Easter. Ash Wednesday begins Lent. Alleluia is not used in the liturgy during Lent.

5. Sacred Paschal Triduum

Usual colours: White for the Mass of the Lord’s Supper; red on Good Friday; white or gold at the Easter Vigil.

The three-day celebration from the evening Mass of the Lord’s Supper on Holy Thursday through Easter Sunday. It commemorates Jesus’ Passion, death and Resurrection. Good Friday is not a Mass.

6. Easter Time

Usual colour: White or gold; red on Pentecost.

Fifty days celebrating the Resurrection, from Easter Sunday through Pentecost. The Ascension is celebrated during this season on the date set by the local calendar.

7. Ordinary Time (second part)

Usual colour: Green.

Resumes after Pentecost and continues until the beginning of Advent. The Church reflects on Christ’s teaching and the life of discipleship.

Liturgical colours and their significance

Colours are used on vestments and other liturgical items to express the character of a celebration. The proper colour can depend on the feast, rite and local calendar; follow your parish’s approved practice.

White

Joy, light, purity and resurrection. Common at Christmas and Easter, and for celebrations of the Lord (other than his Passion), Mary, angels and saints who were not martyrs.

Gold

Festive splendour. In places where permitted, gold or silver vestments may be used for especially solemn celebrations in place of other colours, except violet or black where those are prescribed.

Red

The Holy Spirit’s fire and the blood of Christ and martyrs. Used on Palm Sunday, Good Friday, Pentecost, and celebrations of martyrs.

Violet / Purple

Preparation, penance and conversion. Used during Advent and Lent; it may also be used for Masses for the Dead where permitted.

Green

Hope, life and growth in faith. Used during Ordinary Time.

Rose

A sign of joy within a penitential season. May be used on the Third Sunday of Advent (Gaudete) and Fourth Sunday of Lent (Laetare).

Black

A sign of mourning. It may be used at Masses for the Dead where permitted by local liturgical norms; white or violet is also commonly used.

Blue

Often associated devotionally with the Blessed Virgin Mary, but it is not a universal Roman Rite liturgical colour. It may be used only where a particular indult or local permission allows it.

How the liturgical years are labelled

  • Sunday readings — Years A, B and C: the Sunday Gospel cycle generally highlights Matthew in Year A, Mark in Year B and Luke in Year C; John appears especially during Easter and other seasons. The cycle repeats every three years.
  • Weekday readings — Years I and II: weekday readings in Ordinary Time generally follow a two-year cycle. Year I is used in odd-numbered calendar years and Year II in even-numbered years, with liturgical-calendar details observed.
  • Solemnities, feasts and memorials: celebrations have different ranks. The rank helps determine which prayers, readings and liturgical rules apply.

Server reminder: do not decide the colour from the season alone. A particular solemnity, funeral, wedding or local celebration may have its own rules. Check the parish sacristy calendar or ask the priest/coordinator.

Good habits for serving Mass

  • Arrive early enough to prepare and receive instructions.
  • Wear the vestments your parish has assigned and make sure they are neat.
  • Know your place, route and role before Mass begins.
  • Walk calmly, stand attentively and avoid unnecessary movement or talking.
  • Handle sacred vessels and books carefully, as your parish has taught you.
  • Follow the priest, deacon and parish coordinator rather than guessing what to do.
  • Keep phones away during the liturgy unless an authorised role requires one.
  • If you make a mistake, stay calm and continue reverently.
  • Learn the responses and prayers used in your parish.
  • Remember: serving is a ministry of prayer, humility and helping the assembly worship.
